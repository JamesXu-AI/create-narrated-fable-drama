# Directing, Performance, and Editorial Craft

Integrate story, performance, picture, sound, design, and editing into one dramatic result. Treat directing choices as executable decisions, not prestige labels or decorative taste.

## Craft basis

Use these primary industry references as the conceptual foundation:

- [DGA Basic Agreement Article 7](https://www.dga.org/contracts/creative-rights/basic-agreement-article-7): integrate all creative aspects of sound and picture into one dramatic and aesthetic whole.
- [AFI Directing Curriculum](https://conservatory.afi.com/directing-curriculum/): begin with script analysis; translate text and subtext through performance, visual storytelling, blocking, camera, and editing; motivate every choice.
- [AFI Directing Learning Outcomes](https://conservatory.afi.com/learning-outcomes/): use action, performance, and camera to control information, emotion, drama, character objective, and narrative point of view.

## Decision hierarchy

Make choices in this order:

```text
story/theme -> audience point of view -> character objective and performance ->
blocking and attention -> camera/light/design/sound -> edit rhythm -> generation mode
```

For every major choice, record:

```text
dramatic reason -> audience effect -> observable execution -> editorial consequence ->
continuity cost -> Seedance instruction -> acceptance test
```

Reject a camera move, line reading, visual flourish, music cue, or cut that has no story function. A beautiful choice that weakens point of view, causality, performance, or editability is not approved.

## Director's book contract

Create a compact project-level director's book inside `storyboard.md`:

```yaml
director_intent:
theme_question_and_not_answer:
genre_promise_and_rules:
audience_alignment_and_point_of_view:
knowledge_reveal_and_withholding_strategy:
episode_emotional_trajectory:
character_performance_arcs:
staging_and_distance_thesis:
visual_thesis:
sound_and_silence_thesis:
editorial_rhythm_thesis:
intentional_rule_breaks:
ending_experience:
```

Use one governing statement per field. Do not write several contradictory styles. Translate terms such as `cinematic`, `premium`, `epic`, or `Hollywood` into specific story-serving choices before approving them.

## Audience point of view

For every scene and decisive beat, state:

```yaml
audience_follows:
audience_knows:
audience_suspects:
audience_must_not_see_yet:
attention_target:
reveal_trigger:
expected_emotional_change:
```

Choose whether the audience discovers information with, before, or after a character. Bind that choice to coverage, off-screen sound, prop visibility, reaction timing, and edit order. Do not reveal evidence in a wide shot before the scripted discovery or hide information the audience must read.

## Direct playable performance

Direct an actor through objective and action, not an emotional result:

```text
given circumstance -> objective -> obstacle -> playable verb/tactic -> physical behavior ->
vocal action -> listener effect -> adjustment -> changed state
```

Use verbs such as `test`, `corner`, `deflect`, `invite`, `punish`, `conceal`, or `bargain`. Convert `be sad`, `act angry`, or `look cinematic` into behavior, breath, gaze, timing, distance, voice, and prop use.

For each decisive performance beat, specify:

- What the actor perceives and when.
- What the actor wants from the other person now.
- What tactic begins the beat and what forces it to change.
- What must remain private versus readable to the audience.
- The listener's active task.
- A smaller adjustment if the first direction becomes broad or theatrical.

Do not over-choreograph routine lines. Protect spontaneity by fixing intention, causal order, and result state while allowing low-risk microbehavior to vary.

## Stage attention and power

Block characters, props, architecture, and camera as one attention system. Track:

```text
who owns the space -> who can move -> who is trapped -> who controls the prop ->
who sees whom -> what the audience notices first -> what changes the power geometry
```

Use entrances, exits, distance changes, sitting/standing, thresholds, foreground occlusion, and shared or denied eye contact as dramatic actions. Establish geography before breaking it. Preserve a readable path from the prior pose and weight distribution.

## Design editorial rhythm

Plan the finished cut before compiling source clips:

- Hold when performance, concealment, or discomfort is the event.
- Cut when information, tactic, power, point of view, or attention changes.
- Cut to a listener only when the listener becomes the story.
- Use action, gaze, sound, occlusion, or composition as a motivated transition.
- Preserve overlaps and clean handles needed to adjust timing.
- Vary shot duration by dramatic pressure rather than generating equal-length clips.
- Track setup/payoff, visual motifs, sound motifs, and unresolved questions across the full cut.

Record:

```yaml
scene_rhythm_shape: accumulate | accelerate | suspend | rupture | release
hold_points:
motivated_cut_points:
reaction_priority:
audio_bridge_plan:
coverage_safety:
outgoing_editorial_handle:
```

## Create the directed scene

For creation work, turn the analysis into authored material in this order:

1. Choose the scene's governing audience alignment, withheld information, and emotional destination.
2. Give each character an immediate objective, playable tactic progression, physical behavior, vocal action, and active listening task.
3. Stage bodies, distance, thresholds, props, entrances, exits, and power changes through a reachable action path.
4. Choose the strongest finished shot order and camera behavior that reveals those changes; decide where to hold, cut, or stay off-screen, then choose Segment packing for performance, continuity, controllability, reliability, and editability. Minimize count only among effect-equivalent designs; derive parallel/serial execution afterward without changing the creative plan.
5. Design silence, room tone, effects, music, overlaps, reactions, and the final audiovisual handoff.
6. Write these decisions into the screenplay/storyboard action, dialogue, performance beats, and shot treatment before running acceptance.

Do not stop at `the scene needs more tension`, `use a close-up`, or `the listener should react`. Author what creates the tension, which character notices what, the exact reaction, where it plays, what the camera does, and how the cut lands. When several treatments are viable, select the one that best protects theme, point of view, character truth, and the full-cut rhythm.

Default the finished coverage to the closest frame that preserves the beat's
necessary information. Let faces, eye lines, breaths, active hands/paws, wounds,
clues, and listener reactions carry decisive changes. Use a wide frame only when
new geography, scale, full-body mechanics, entrance/exit travel, or relationship
distance is the event. Never solve ensemble continuity by staging the whole cast
frontally in a repeated master; physical presence may continue outside a close
crop.

## Review and repair generated footage

When actual footage is supplied, review in this order:

1. Story and audience comprehension.
2. Performance truth, tactic, listening, and changed state.
3. Shot purpose, attention, and editorial timing.
4. Identity, space, props, light, voice, sound, and boundary continuity.
5. Aesthetic fidelity and technical defects.

Diagnose the first causal failure instead of listing symptoms. Repair the smallest layer that can solve it:

```text
edit/trim -> prompt clarification -> duration or coverage change -> reference/mode change ->
storyboard patch -> scene redesign
```

Do not redesign the story to fix a local camera or continuity failure. Do not call planned footage `observed`.

## Acceptance

Mark `BLOCKER` when:

- Theme, point of view, character objective, and scene result contradict one another.
- The audience receives decisive information at the wrong time.
- Direction gives only emotional adjectives with no playable action for a decisive beat.
- Blocking, camera, and edit order direct attention away from essential story information.
- A scene has activity but no value, relationship, knowledge, danger, or decision change.
- Coverage cannot produce the intended rhythm, reveal, or performance emphasis.
- Coverage repeatedly exposes the whole set and cast like a proscenium stage even
  though the decisive event lives in a face, reaction, or detail.
- Coverage is organized as one generated video per shot even though several consecutive shots share one executable duration, reference/mode contract, and dramatic micro-sequence.
- Coverage, shot order, reactions, camera/light continuity, Segment packing, operation, or reference authority is weakened or redesigned merely to increase parallel throughput or reduce waiting.
- A generated segment restarts a completed beat or invents an unmotivated new beat.

Mark `FIX` when the story remains executable but a choice is generic, decorative, over-directed, weakly motivated, or editorially redundant.

Approve only when every major choice can complete this sentence:

```text
Because [story reason], the audience experiences [effect] through [observable choice],
which hands off to [edit/next state] and passes [acceptance condition].
```
