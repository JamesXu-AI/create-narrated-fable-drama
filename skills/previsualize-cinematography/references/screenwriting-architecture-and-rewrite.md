# Screenwriting Architecture and Rewrite

Build a cinematic story whose structure, characters, scenes, behavior, dialogue, silence, and ending create one causal dramatic experience. Treat structural models as tools, not mandatory formulas.

## Craft basis

Use these primary craft references:

- [AFI Screenwriting Curriculum](https://conservatory.afi.com/screenwriting-curriculum/): emphasize dramatic structure, cinematic action, character reality, development, feedback, and rewriting.
- [AFI Learning Outcomes](https://conservatory.afi.com/learning-outcomes/): develop structure, character, emotional arc, dialogue, dynamic scenes, and plot in service of drama.
- [BAFTA/BFI Andrew Bovell Lecture](https://www.bafta.org/media-centre/press-releases/bafta-bfi-screenwriters-lecture-series-andrew-bovell/): evoke pace, rhythm, tone, vivid behavior, what is said, and what remains unsaid.
- [Sundance Short-Film Screenwriting](https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/Screenwriting-Crafting-Your-Short-Film): build character, dramatic problem, conflict, tension, action, dialogue, and achievable short-form structure.

## Story contract

Record before scene work:

```yaml
premise:
theme_question:
genre_and_audience_promise:
protagonist_external_want:
protagonist_internal_need_or_fear:
central_opposition:
stakes_and_clock:
inciting_disruption:
major_irreversible_turns:
climax_choice:
ending_payoff_or_aftertaste:
emotional_arc:
```

Use acts, sequences, chapters, loops, or another structure only when they clarify pursuit and change. Do not force a three-act template onto material that uses another coherent dramatic form.

## Character engine

For every principal character, track:

```yaml
public_want:
private_need_or_fear:
misbelief_or_protective_strategy:
stakes:
knowledge_and_secrets:
contradiction:
pressure_behavior:
speech_and_silence_pattern:
relationship_arcs:
choice_that_proves_change_or_refusal:
```

Create action from conflicting wants, knowledge, values, timing, and willingness. Do not manufacture conflict that disappears when people communicate normally unless avoidance itself is character truth.

## Causality, escalation, and payoff

Build the chain:

```text
cause -> pursuit -> obstacle -> tactic -> result -> new information/pressure ->
harder choice -> irreversible consequence -> payoff
```

Track:

- Setup, reminder, complication, and payoff.
- Promises, threats, lies, questions, debts, evidence, wounds, and missing objects.
- What each character knows and when.
- Escalation of cost, intimacy, exposure, time pressure, and moral compromise.
- Reversals that change the plan or meaning, not merely add noise.

Reject coincidence that resolves the central problem, payoff without setup, reaction without perception, and action based on impossible knowledge.

## Scene craft

Give every scene:

```yaml
why_now:
incoming_pressure:
point_of_view:
objective_and_obstacle:
tactic_progression:
audience_information_plan:
setup_or_payoff:
turn:
value_change:
outgoing_pressure:
next_question:
```

Enter after the scene's obvious preliminaries and leave after its dramatic result becomes readable. Remove greetings, travel, repetition, explanation, and aftermath that do not change pressure or meaning. Preserve stillness when it is the active dramatic event.

## Cinematic action, subtext, and silence

Write what can be seen or heard. Reveal interior life through choice, behavior, rhythm, space, objects, contradiction, failed speech, and reaction.

Use this hierarchy:

```text
behavior/action -> image/sound -> implication/subtext -> dialogue -> explanation
```

Do not make dialogue state visible action, shared history both speakers know, or emotions the performance can carry. Give silence an owner, objective, and observable behavior.

## Dialogue voice

Create a distinct voice bible:

```yaml
vocabulary_and_register:
sentence_length_and_rhythm:
directness_or_evasion:
humor_and_metaphor:
status_behavior:
habitual_defense:
what_the_character_never_says:
how_voice_changes_under_pressure:
```

Every line must have an owner, addressee, trigger, tactic, subtext, and result. Preserve speakability, breath, interruption logic, and listener activity. Let decisive lines land; do not surround them with explanatory echoes.

## Draft the professional scene

After locking the story and character engines, write each scene rather than returning the engines as analysis:

1. Enter on the event that makes the scene unavoidable now.
2. Establish the point-of-view character's immediate perception and pressure through visible/audible behavior.
3. Let objectives collide through tactics, props, spatial choices, silence, and complete character-specific dialogue.
4. Escalate by changing cost, knowledge, leverage, intimacy, time, or moral compromise.
5. Land a turn that changes fact, choice, relationship, danger, or physical state.
6. Show the listener and environment absorb the turn, then leave on outgoing pressure.

Write concrete action prose and exact lines. Do not deliver only a beat outline when the requested artifact is a professional storyboard or script. When the input is sparse, create the missing cinematic behavior, subtext, line phrasing, listener response, and scene punctuation while preserving the supplied premise and outcome.

## Rewrite passes

Rewrite in separate passes:

1. Premise, genre promise, ending, and runtime.
2. Causality, turns, escalation, and setup/payoff.
3. Character wants, knowledge, contradiction, and relationship arcs.
4. Scene purpose, point of view, turn, and value change.
5. Cinematic behavior, action, props, and silence.
6. Dialogue ownership, subtext, voice, and speakability.
7. Performance and visual opportunities.
8. Duration, production feasibility, and Seedance segmentation.
9. Continuity, repetition, theme, and final compression.

Do not polish dialogue while the scene or sequence lacks causality. Record every substantive approved-line change as `original -> revised -> reason`.

## Traceability

Maintain:

```text
theme/genre promise -> sequence pursuit -> scene value change -> character choice ->
performance beat -> visual/audio treatment -> dialogue line -> payoff
```

Create setup/payoff and knowledge ledgers:

```text
item -> setup -> who knows -> escalation/reminder -> payoff/abandonment -> status
```

No scene, action, line, motif, or prop should survive solely because it is stylish. It must protect story, character, performance, rhythm, continuity, or audience comprehension.

## Acceptance

Mark `BLOCKER` when:

- The premise, climax choice, and ending do not answer the same dramatic problem.
- A sequence or scene has no causal trigger, pursuit, turn, or changed state.
- A character acts on impossible knowledge or reacts before perception.
- A payoff lacks setup, or a required setup disappears without intent.
- Dialogue replaces action, subtext, or necessary listener behavior.
- Characters share one generic voice or repeat the same tactic without escalation.
- The story cannot fit the target runtime at performable speed.
- A rewrite silently changes approved meaning, speaker, or relationship outcome.

Mark `FIX` when the story is executable but predictable, repetitive, over-explained, weakly escalated, tonally generic, or insufficiently visual.

Approve only when story structure, character choice, cinematic behavior, dialogue, performance opportunity, runtime, and ending payoff support one another.
