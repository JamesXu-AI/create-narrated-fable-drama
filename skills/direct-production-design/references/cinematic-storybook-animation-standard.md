# Cinematic Storybook Animation Standard

## Look

Use the exact conversational Visual Style recorded in `screenplay.md`. Default to
`3D Healing Animation` only when the user supplied no style. Translate the chosen
style into coherent material, light, depth, palette, shape language, and
performance decisions. Do not imitate a studio, franchise, artist, renderer, or
protected identity.

## Characters

- Preserve the Story's human, animal, fantasy, or living-object identity.
- Author one coherent body topology and locomotion model per character.
- Keep face, age, scale, skin/fur/material, hair, costume, accessories, and
  temporary state stable.
- Use one subject per identity image. Do not use turnarounds, collages, or repeated
  views that can become duplicate performers.
- Keep facial features and hands/paws readable enough for close dramatic coverage.
- Exclude duplicate bodies, extra limbs, fused anatomy, mirror doubles, background
  copies, typography, logos, and watermarks.

## Environments

- Treat each Location as a dressed dramatic set with stable layout, entrances,
  exits, routes, interaction points, fixed furniture, landmarks, population,
  season, weather, time, light, and atmosphere.
- Reuse the same Location master for unchanged revisits.
- Keep framing and embedded-story worlds visually distinct when the screenplay
  separates them.
- Do not include a framing storyteller inside an embedded-world Location merely
  because that character's voice continues over it.

## Image grammar

- Compose identity assets on seamless pure white.
- Compose Location masters as useful 16:9 environment authorities, not finished
  Shots and not generic backgrounds.
- Use motivated soft key and fill, controlled highlights, physically coherent
  materials, and clear foreground/midground/background separation.
- Keep enough texture and identity detail to survive close and medium-close video
  generation.
- Avoid excessive saturation, crushed blacks, clipped highlights, hard artificial
  rim light, unreadable clutter, and pseudo-writing.

## Culture

Use the screenplay's target country only for delivery and cultural safety unless
the Story itself is set there. Do not add flags, monuments, religious symbols,
clothing, architecture, or stereotypes unsupported by the Story.

## Acceptance

Accept an asset only when it is a stable reusable authority, matches the
screenplay-owned identity and world facts, contains no unintended performer, and
can be referenced by Seedance without implying a wrong narrative presence.
